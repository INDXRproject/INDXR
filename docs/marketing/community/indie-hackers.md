# Indie Hackers — bouwverhaal (Khidr plaatst)

**Titel:**
I spent €540 on Google Ads for my transcription SaaS and got zero sales. Here's what I learned.

**Tekst:**

I'm a solo developer from the Netherlands building INDXR (https://indxr.ai/?utm_source=indiehackers): you paste a YouTube link or upload a recording and get a transcript with speakers and timestamps that you can keep in a searchable library and export to Markdown, subtitles or RAG-ready JSON. Pay per minute, no subscription.

Here's an honest look at the last two months.

**Round 1 (Aug–Sep): account + 50 free credits.** €463 on Google Search ads, 281 clicks, 22 signups, 14 people actually transcribed something. Zero purchases. Looking at the data, the 50 free credits covered 100% of what people needed. I gave away the one job they came for.

**Round 2 (Oct–Nov): pay per file, no account.** I built a quick checkout: upload, see the price, read a free preview, pay for that one file (€0.10/min, €2 minimum). €77 on ads, 58 visitors, 12 got a price. Most uploaded short phone clips, saw €2, and left. Zero purchases.

**What I think went wrong:** people searching "video to text" have one small job and plenty of free options. My product only really makes sense for people who process a lot of long content over and over: students and researchers building a knowledge base, podcasters, people feeding transcripts into RAG. Those people don't search "video to text". I was buying the wrong traffic.

**What I'm doing now:** ads off. Instead I'm trying to reach the people who'd use it repeatedly, where they already are, and I'm measuring one thing: does someone come back with a second source within 30 days?

If you've sold to students, researchers or knowledge workers: where did your first paying users come from? And if you try INDXR, I'd love blunt feedback on what would make it worth paying for.
